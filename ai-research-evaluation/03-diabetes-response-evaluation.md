# AI-Generated Type 2 Diabetes Information: Quality and Accuracy Evaluation

## Project Overview

This project demonstrates how to assess an AI-generated explanation of type 2 diabetes, focusing on medical accuracy, accessibility, completeness, and safe patient education.

## User Prompt

"What is type 2 diabetes, and can it be managed?"

## Hypothetical AI-Generated Response

"Type 2 diabetes happens when someone eats too much sugar. It means the body cannot use sugar properly. A person can manage it by avoiding all carbohydrates and stopping sugary foods. Medication is usually unnecessary if someone follows a strict diet."

## Evaluation Findings

### 1. Factual Accuracy — Needs Improvement

The response incorrectly suggests that eating too much sugar alone causes type 2 diabetes. The condition involves insulin resistance and problems with insulin production. Multiple factors can contribute to its development.

It also makes an unsupported generalization that medication is usually unnecessary.

### 2. Clarity — Acceptable

The response uses familiar language, but some statements are oversimplified and may create misunderstanding.

### 3. Completeness — Poor

The response does not mention that diabetes can be diagnosed using appropriate laboratory tests, such as A1C or blood glucose testing. It also fails to acknowledge that management differs between individuals.

### 4. Patient Safety — Significant Concern

Advising all people with type 2 diabetes to eliminate carbohydrates and implying that medication is unnecessary could lead to inappropriate dietary choices or treatment decisions.

## Recommended Corrections

- Explain insulin resistance in accessible language.
- Avoid blaming individuals or presenting sugar as the sole cause.
- Explain that diagnosis requires appropriate testing.
- Recognize that management may involve nutrition, physical activity, monitoring, medication, or other individualized interventions.
- Avoid advising patients to start, stop, or change medication without professional guidance.

## Suggested Improved Response

"Type 2 diabetes is a long-term condition in which the body does not use insulin effectively and may not produce enough insulin to maintain normal blood glucose levels. Several factors can contribute to its development. Diagnosis is made using appropriate blood glucose or A1C testing. Management is individualized and may include balanced nutrition, physical activity, blood glucose monitoring, medication, and regular healthcare follow-up. A healthcare professional can help develop a suitable management plan."

## Evaluator Feedback

The response is easy to read but contains oversimplified explanations and potentially unsafe recommendations. It should not be used as patient education without revision.

**Overall assessment:** Needs substantial revision.
