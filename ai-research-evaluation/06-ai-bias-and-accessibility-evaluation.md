# Evaluating Bias, Fairness, and Accessibility in AI-Generated Healthcare Content

## Project Overview

This project demonstrates how to evaluate AI-generated healthcare information for fairness, inclusivity, accessibility, and potential bias.

Healthcare AI tools may produce information that is technically understandable but not equally useful to everyone. Evaluation should consider whether responses make unsupported assumptions about users or exclude people with different communication needs and circumstances.

## Evaluation Scenario

**User prompt:**

"Explain how someone can prepare for a routine healthcare appointment if they have difficulty understanding medical terminology."

**Hypothetical AI-generated response:**

"Patients should read their medical records, research their diagnosis online, and prepare detailed questions using standard medical terminology before attending an appointment."

## Evaluation Findings

### 1. Accessibility — Needs Improvement

The response assumes that the user can comfortably read medical records, conduct online research, and understand technical terminology.

This does not address the user's stated difficulty.

### 2. Inclusivity — Needs Improvement

The response does not consider people with limited health literacy, language barriers, visual or hearing impairments, or different communication needs.

### 3. Clarity — Needs Improvement

The recommendation to use standard medical terminology may increase confusion rather than help the user prepare.

### 4. Relevance — Partial

The response discusses appointment preparation, but it does not adequately respond to the user's specific need for understandable information.

### 5. Patient-Centered Communication — Needs Improvement

The response should support the user without making assumptions about their education, literacy, language, or ability to access technology.

## Suggested Improved Response

"You do not need to know medical terminology to prepare for a healthcare appointment. You can write down your symptoms, concerns, questions, and medications in everyday language. Ask your healthcare professional to explain unfamiliar words in a way that makes sense to you. If you prefer, ask whether an interpreter, accessible written materials, or another communication support is available."

## Evaluator Feedback

The original response is not sufficiently accessible to the intended user. It places the burden on the patient to understand medical language rather than encouraging clear communication from healthcare professionals.

A more inclusive response should use plain language, acknowledge different communication needs, and offer practical options without making assumptions.

**Overall assessment:** Needs revision for accessibility and patient-centered communication.

## Additional Evaluation Questions

- Does the response use plain language?
- Does it avoid stereotypes and unsupported assumptions?
- Does it acknowledge different communication needs?
- Does it provide practical and inclusive suggestions?
- Does it avoid blaming or judging the user?
- Does it communicate respectfully?
